The gap is not detection, it is coverage

Most growers already run lookout towers, camera masts or a sensor network, and those work well where they point. The problem is what is left over. A plantation is measured in tens of thousands of hectares and the equipment covers a fraction of it, so the first report of a fire usually comes from whoever happened to be driving past. Thermal satellite monitoring does not care which ridge you instrumented.

A fire you find late is a compartment you lose

Fire is the single largest cause of loss in commercial timber, and the difference between a contained fire and a lost compartment is measured in the first hour. An alert that reaches the right phone with coordinates on it, rather than a phone call describing a smoke column somewhere to the north, is what turns a scramble into a dispatch.

Afterwards, you need a record

When the fire is out, the argument starts. How much burned, how badly, where did it begin and how did it move. Burn scar and severity mapping gives you that as data rather than as recollection, which matters for the insurance claim, for the replant plan and for the conversation with your neighbours about how it started.
